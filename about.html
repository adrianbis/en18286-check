About the project

Making the EU AI Act's quality management requirements easier to check

Providers of high-risk AI systems must run a quality management system under Article 17 of the EU AI Act. FprEN 18286:2026 is the harmonised standard that sets out what that system needs to contain. It's detailed, and for many teams the first hard question is simply: where do we stand?

This tool gives organisations a structured, private way to answer that question — and a gap report to take to the next conversation with their team, their auditor or their board.

It was built by a team of three as a project at the ML4Good AI safety bootcamp.

How the assessment works

Three areas

AI risk management and fundamental rights; data governance and management (§ 8.5); and testing, where verification against specifications is assessed separately from validation against intended purpose (§ 8.4). Each question maps to specific clauses.

Applicability first

We start by asking whether you provide or deploy AI. The requirements are mainly provider obligations, so a deployer isn't marked down for activities its provider controls. Each area also checks whether a process exists before going into detail.

Scored on what applies

Answers are scored from absent to established. "Select all" questions measure coverage of the activities that apply to you. "Not sure" and "not applicable" are reported separately and never counted as non-compliance.

Private by design

There is no server and no database. Your answers stay in your browser; a share link carries the results inside the link itself.

Limitations

This is an indicative self-assessment covering selected requirements of FprEN 18286:2026. It is not a conformity assessment, a certification, an audit or legal advice, and a good result here does not mean a system complies with the EU AI Act. It does not yet cover the additional requirements for AI systems that continue to learn after release (§ 8.8). The standard is a final draft and its clause numbering may change on publication. The questions reflect our reading of the standard; they don't reproduce it, and the standard itself remains the authoritative source.
